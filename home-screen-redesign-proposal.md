# Bandhuu Home Screen — Redesign Proposal

---

## The Central Idea

Before touching a single pixel, the question to answer is: **what should this screen feel like?**

Bandhuu is not a social media app. It is closer to finding a familiar face in a foreign crowd. The emotional register is warmth, recognition, belonging — the quiet joy of hearing your hometown name in a city of strangers.

The redesign is built around one idea: **an editorial community publication, not a social feed.** Think of a local newspaper or a community zine that lands on your doorstep — it has a voice, it respects its readers' intelligence, it values content over chrome. That is the feeling this screen should deliver. Not sterile. Not corporate. Not generic startup-blue.

Every design decision below flows from that premise.

---

## 1. Visual Identity

### 1.1 Color Palette

The palette is built around warmth and cultural resonance. No cold blues as primary surfaces. No pure white backgrounds.

**Background / Surface**
- Page background: a warm off-white, the color of uncoated paper — not cream, not grey, just slightly warm. Think of an aged broadsheet. This immediately separates the app from the sterile white of every generic app.
- Cards sit on this background as slightly brighter off-white surfaces with an almost imperceptible warmth shift. The distinction between card and background is achieved through very soft shadow and barely visible warmth difference — not harsh borders.

**Primary Accent — Terracotta / Deep Saffron**
- One dominant action color. A deep, rich terracotta-orange — the color of a fired clay pot, a marigold garland, a Diwali diya flame. This is not generic orange. It is darker, more deliberate, more Indian.
- Used for: primary action buttons, active tab indicator, category highlights, like state, RSVP button.
- This color has cultural memory. It is not chosen arbitrarily.

**Secondary Accent — Monsoon Green**
- A deep, lush forest green — the color of monsoon-soaked leaves. Not mint, not teal.
- Used exclusively for: "joined/confirmed" states (RSVP joined, connection sent/confirmed), success indicators.
- Keeps the palette from feeling monochromatic while staying warm.

**Text**
- Primary text: a warm near-black (not pure #000000 — it's too harsh). Think ink on paper.
- Secondary text: warm mid-grey for timestamps, metadata, descriptions.
- Muted text: lighter warm grey for counts, placeholders.

**Category Color System**
Each post category has its own dedicated color family used only as an accent (not background fills on cards):
- General → warm amber
- Flatmate / Housing → slate blue
- Travelmate / Trip → teal
- Hangouts → soft coral / rose
- Help / Questions → muted violet

**Principle**: the palette should feel like it was chosen by a person with taste, not generated by an algorithm. No gradients. No glass-morphism. No dark mode as an afterthought.

---

### 1.2 Typography

The single most powerful decision in this redesign is **mixing a serif and a sans-serif.** This is what gives editorial identity and makes the screen feel like a crafted publication.

**Display / Title font: a humanist serif**
A font like Fraunces, DM Serif Display, or Playfair Display — with personality, slightly literary. Used exclusively for post titles and meetup titles. This is the voice of the community. Bold, confident, readable.

**UI font: a clean geometric sans**
A font like Inter or DM Sans — neutral, highly legible at small sizes. Used for all metadata, labels, buttons, search, filter chips, counts, timestamps, section headers.

**Scale**
- Post title: large, heavy serif — the hero of the card
- Meetup title: slightly smaller than post title but same serif weight
- Author name: medium-weight sans, comfortable reading size
- Body text / description: regular weight sans, loose line height for readability
- Metadata (time, category label, count): small, all-caps for labels, regular case for numbers
- Buttons: uppercase, tight letter-spacing, medium-weight sans

**Why this matters**: Currently everything is sans-serif at varying weights. The serif title creates instant hierarchy — your eye goes to the title first, then the author, then the body. No hierarchy rules needed, the type does the work.

---

### 1.3 Space and Rhythm

The grid is built on 4px base units. Key spacing decisions:

- 20px horizontal page margin — content sits inside a comfortable channel
- Cards have 16px internal padding — generous but not wasteful
- Between cards: 10px gap — they breathe without feeling disconnected
- Section headers get 24px top breathing room
- The bottom action bar of cards (like/dislike/comment) has 14px top padding separated by a hairline — not a thick divider, just a suggestion of separation

---

### 1.4 Cards and Surfaces

**No heavy border + shadow combination.** That combination creates visual noise.

Cards use: very soft shadow (2px vertical, 8px blur, very low opacity warm tone) + a subtle 1px border in a barely-visible warm tone. On the off-white background, this creates a sense of cards being "placed" on the surface — not floating.

The card corners are rounded but not excessively so — friendly, not bubbly.

## 2. The Header

### 2.1 What Changes

The current header is purely functional. The redesign makes it feel like the masthead of the publication — the thing that orients you when you open the app.

### 2.2 Structure

**Top line — App identity / Greeting**
A single line at the very top of the sticky header, before the tab switcher. Two options, and I'd go with the second:

Option A: Just the app name "Bandhuu" in the serif font, left-aligned.
Option B: A contextual greeting — "Good morning, Deepak" in the regular weight serif. Warm, personal, immediately establishes the app's character.

This greeting line is small and understated — it is not a large hero header. It sets the tone without consuming space.

**Tab Switcher**
Not underline tabs. Not pill tabs with background fill.

The tab switcher is two large, bold labels side by side: "Posts" and "Meetups." The active tab label is in the primary terracotta accent color, full weight. The inactive tab is muted grey. Between them, no separator — just negative space.

Below the active tab label, there is a short, thick accent line (not a full-width underline — more like a deliberate brushstroke, slightly wider than the word itself). This accent line animates with the swipe transition, sliding from Posts to Meetups as you swipe.

**Why not pill tabs?** Pill tabs look like a default iOS component. Text tabs with an accent underline look like an intentional design decision.

**Search + Filter Controls**
Below the tab switcher. Full width, horizontal row.

The search bar: white background, soft rounded corners, subtle shadow that lifts it slightly off the page background. A small magnifying glass icon on the left in the muted color. Placeholder text in muted tone. The clear button (×) appears on the right when text is entered.

When focused, the search bar's border subtly shifts to the accent color — a soft, warm glow. Not a bright electric blue outline.

The filter button: a small square-ish icon button to the right of the search bar. Contains a sliders/tuning icon. When filters are active, the button fills with the terracotta accent color and the icon inverts to white. No badge count — the fill itself is the indicator.

**Meetups sub-controls** (visible only on Meetups tab):
Below the search bar, three pill-shaped status filters (Upcoming / Completed / All) align left. These pills are compact and uniform. Active pill: dark ink fill, white text. Inactive: pale background, muted text. Compact, not oversized.

The date filter button sits on the right end of the same row — calendar icon, same styling as the posts filter button.

When a date is selected, a small chip appears below the pills row with the date and a × to clear. The chip is styled in the secondary blue/slate tone to distinguish it from action elements.

**Transition between modes**
When switching from Posts to Meetups, the search bar does not animate — it stays in place. Only the controls below it (the meetups sub-tabs row) slide in from the right, accompanying the swipe gesture. This creates a feeling that the search bar is persistent infrastructure while the mode-specific tools appear.

## 3. Posts Tab — Feed Design

### 3.1 Section: Upcoming Meetups Carousel

**Renamed from "Upcoming Meetups" to "Happening Soon"** — shorter, more conversational.

The section header: "Happening Soon" in the section title treatment (bold sans, small, all-caps with generous letter-spacing) on the left. "View all" as a small text link on the right — not "See all →", just "View all" in the accent color. Clean.

**Carousel card redesign — portrait orientation**

The current carousel uses short landscape cards. I'd switch to portrait cards (roughly 150px wide × 210px tall). This is a major change — portrait event cards feel more like event posters. The image fills the top 55% of the card. The bottom 45% holds the content.

**Inside a carousel card:**

Top half (image):
- Cover image fills the space with object-fit: cover
- A subtle dark gradient overlay from center-to-bottom of the image (not a harsh band — a soft vignette)
- Overlaid on the image, bottom-left: the date in bold white type ("MON 2 JUN") in a small all-caps sans — no background pill, the gradient provides enough contrast

Bottom half (card body, white background):
- Top: the time, in a small all-caps muted sans label ("6:30 PM")
- Middle: meetup title in the serif font, bold, up to 2 lines, truncated with ellipsis
- Bottom row: spots remaining on the left ("3 left" with a people icon) + RSVP action on the right

**Hometown tag**: if present, it sits just below the time as a small inline badge — a tiny terracotta dot followed by "For Patna" in small bold sans. It reads like a whispered aside: "this one's for you."

**RSVP button states inside carousel card:**
- "RSVP" — small solid pill, terracotta fill, white text
- "JOINED" — small outlined pill, monsoon green border and text
- "YOURS" — small flat label, muted text, no border — feels like a note to self

**Horizontal scroll behavior**: the cards peek slightly off the right edge of the screen to signal there are more. The leftmost card aligns to the 20px page margin.

---

### 3.2 The Posts Feed

Section header: "Your Feed" — or even just no label at all, letting the first card be the implicit start of the feed. Removing the label reduces noise and signals confidence.

**The Post Card**

This is the most important design surface on the screen. The card redesign rethinks the hierarchy fundamentally.

**Author row**

Avatar: 38px circular photo, no visible border (the circular mask is enough). Sits on the left.

Name: bold sans, comfortable size. Directly next to the avatar.
Time ago: muted small text, directly below the name.

The connect button moves from being a floating icon button to a **small outlined pill** — text-based, not just an icon. This is intentional: icon-only buttons require mental parsing. A small pill with "+ Connect" text removes ambiguity. But it must be small — it's a secondary action.

States:
- Not connected: small outlined pill, terracotta border + text, label "+ Connect"
- Request sent: small solid pill, monsoon green fill, "Requested" in white — shows confidence that the action happened
- Connected: small outlined pill, muted border, "Connected" label — the label tells you the status without requiring an icon interpretation
- Own post: no button shown — silence is cleaner than a disabled element

**Category indicator — the left accent stripe**

Instead of a floating chip in the card body, the category is expressed as a 3px vertical colored stripe along the left edge of the entire card body (below the author row hairline). This stripe runs the full height of the title + body area.

The category label text appears just above the title as a tiny all-caps sans in the stripe's color.

Why: The stripe is a persistent, non-intrusive category signal that doesn't consume horizontal space or interrupt reading flow. You glance at the stripe and instantly know the category. It's an editorial magazine technique — colored section rules.

**Post title**

The largest, most prominent text on the card. Serif font, bold weight. This is the headline. If the title is short, it gets even more visual space through generous line-height.

**Post body**

Regular weight sans, comfortable reading size, relaxed line height. Truncated at 3 lines (not 4 — tighter truncation creates more visual rhythm across the feed, since more cards are visible above the fold).

"Read more" link: terracotta accent color, no underline, bold weight. When expanded, it becomes "Show less."

**Post image (optional)**

Full-width inside the card, 4:3 aspect ratio (not 16:9 — 4:3 feels more natural for community photos). Rounded corners on all four sides. Appears after the body text.

**Post action bar**

Separated from the body by a single hairline (not a full divider, just a suggestion). The bar has generous vertical padding — it should feel easy to tap without crowding the content above.

Left group: Like icon + count · Dislike icon + count · Comment icon + count
Right: Bookmark icon

All icons are outlined by default, filled when active. No button backgrounds or containers around the action icons — they are naked icons with counts. Clean.

Active like state: icon fills in terracotta, count in terracotta.
Active dislike state: icon fills in muted blue-grey, count in same tone.
Active bookmark: icon fills in the accent blue/slate tone.

The counts animate when toggled — a brief +1/-1 number tick animation (not a jumping icon — just the number increments/decrements with a quick slide-up/slide-down).

## 4. Meetups Tab — Full Design

### 4.1 Status Filters

Already described in the header section. The three pills (Upcoming / Completed / All) default to Upcoming. The active pill uses the ink fill so it doesn't look like a random color is being used for state — it looks like a deliberate design choice.

### 4.2 The Full Meetup Card

The meetup card is distinct from the post card — it leans more toward an **event flyer** than a text post. The image is the hero.

**Image section (top of card)**

Full card width. Height: 180px. Slight rounded corners on top corners only (bottom corners are square, because the card body below them is straight).

Overlaid on the image:
- Top-left: Date badge — not just a pill, but structured like a **tear-off calendar tile**: the day number is large and bold, the month abbreviated below it in smaller text. Example: "2" on top, "JUN" below. The whole tile has a white background with slight opacity, small square shape, moderate border-radius. This feels like a real calendar marker.
- Bottom-right: Spots badge — "4 left" or "Full" — small semi-transparent white pill, dark text. Fades toward zero: 4+ spots feels normal, 1–3 spots gets a subtle warm tint (quiet urgency without screaming "LIMITED TIME OFFER").

**Card body (below image)**

Top row: Time ("6:30 PM" in all-caps muted sans) + hometown tag if applicable
The hometown tag here: slightly more prominent than on carousel cards — a small warm badge with a location pin emoji or icon + "For Patna folks." The warmth of this tag should feel like a hand on the shoulder.

Title: serif font, bold. Slightly smaller than post titles since there's more surrounding info.

Description: truncatable at 2 lines. "Read more" same treatment as posts.

Location row: a small horizontal pill — location pin icon (in the secondary blue accent) + venue name. Styled as a pill with pale blue tint background. Compact, single-line, max-width fills available space.

Footer row:
- Left: member facepile or simple "4/10 members" count with a group icon. If I can show 3–4 small overlapping circular avatars of joined members + a "+N more" count, that is far more compelling than just a number. It makes the meetup feel alive and attended, not just an abstract slot count.
- Right: RSVP action button

**RSVP button states (full meetup card)**:
- "Join Meetup" — solid terracotta fill, white bold text, pill shape. Primary action, confident.
- "Joined ✓" — solid monsoon green fill, white text "Joined". Tapping this leaves the meetup. The "Joined" label communicates success clearly without needing a checkmark icon.
- "Full" — flat, muted grey background, muted text. Visually recedes — it's not actionable, so it shouldn't compete for attention.
- "Your Meetup" — outlined pill, terracotta outline + text, no fill. Reads as informational ownership without implying a tap action.

## 5. Filter Bottom Sheet

The filter sheet is a full-height bottom sheet (85% of screen height). Design decisions:

**Handle and header**
Thin drag handle at top, centered. Below it: "Filter Posts" as a large, bold sans title on the left. A small text "Reset" in the terracotta accent on the right. The X close button disappears — the handle communicates dismiss, and the "Reset" text link is more discoverable as an explicit action.

**Sections**

Each filter section has a small all-caps section label with generous top spacing. Sections are:

1. **Location** — two stacked text inputs (Hometown city, Current city) with soft backgrounds, each with an icon prefix (home icon, pin icon). Very simple. No fancy UI.

2. **Gender** — three pill chips in a wrapping row: Male / Female / Other.

3. **Sort By** — four pill chips: Newest / Oldest / Most Liked / Most Disliked.

4. **Category** — six pill chips in a wrapping grid: all six categories. Each chip has its category color as the active state highlight instead of a generic accent color. When "Flatmate / Housing" is active, its chip turns slate blue. When "Hangouts" is active, its chip turns coral. This reinforces the category color system and makes the filter sheet feel integrated with the feed.

**Chips design**
Inactive: pale warm background, moderate border, mid-grey text.
Active: white text on the category/section's relevant accent color, no border — the fill is the indicator.

The chip design should NOT look like generic tags — these should feel like confident, clickable labels.

**Apply button**
Full-width, solid terracotta fill, rounded pill shape, "Apply Filters" in bold white uppercase. Sits in a footer bar separated by a hairline. The footer background is white, the button is the clear CTA.

## 6. Empty States

Empty states are personality moments — they tell users who the app is.

**Empty post feed (no posts / no matches)**

A large, centered illustration-style element — not a Material icon, but something warmer. Could be a simple line-art sketch of a neighborhood, a tea glass, or two people talking. Below it:

- A serif headline: "Your people are here." (or "Nothing yet — check back soon.")
- A small sans subtitle: "Posts from your city will appear here."

The illustration should feel like it was drawn by hand — even if it's SVG, it should have slightly organic, imperfect lines.

**Empty meetups (no meetups / no matches)**

If no active filters: "No meetups yet." with a serif subline "Be the first to gather your people." and a subtle hint that meetups can be created from the Post tab.

If filters are active: "Nothing matches." + "Try adjusting your filters" + a "Clear filters" text link in the accent color (not a button — keep the empty state quiet).

## 7. Motion and Interaction Design

### 7.1 Tab Swipe Transition

The swipe between Posts and Meetups uses spring physics — natural deceleration, slight overshoot that corrects back. Not a rubber-band bounce, just organic deceleration. The key is that both pages are pre-rendered, so there is zero flash. The accent underline in the tab switcher slides smoothly as you drag.

### 7.2 Like / Dislike Animation

When a user taps Like: the icon does a quick scale punch (0.85 → 1.15 → 1.0 in ~200ms). The count ticks up with a vertical slide-in animation — the new number slides up from below as the old number slides up and fades out. This is a single micro-animation borrowed from Twitter's like, but softer and warmer.

### 7.3 Bookmark

On tap: the icon scales briefly (same punch as like), then the fill color sweeps in with a short radial or horizontal fill animation. The bookmark filling is satisfying in a way that a simple state-swap is not.

### 7.4 Connect Button

On tap: the pill fades briefly (opacity 1 → 0.4 → 1) during the request, then transitions to the "Requested" state with a short cross-fade. No spinner inside the button — the brief fade communicates loading without mechanical UI.

### 7.5 RSVP

On tap: button fill briefly pulses (opacity flicker) then cross-fades to the "Joined" green state. The snackbar appears from the bottom simultaneously. If the navigation to Messages happens, it is a smooth push transition — not an abrupt jump.

### 7.6 Read More / Show Less

The body text expansion uses a height animation (LayoutAnimation easeInEaseOut is correct) — no cut. The "Read more" text fades out as the text expands, and "Show less" fades in at the new bottom.

### 7.7 Card Entrance (initial load)

When posts load, they don't all appear at once. They stagger in from bottom — each card slides up and fades in with a 40ms offset between cards. Subtle, not theatrical. The first card appears first, then the second, etc. This creates a sense of content appearing naturally rather than dumping onto the screen.

## 8. The Hometown Tag — Special Attention

This element deserves its own section because it is the single most differentiating piece of the entire app.

Every time the hometown tag appears — on a carousel card, on a meetup card, on a post author row (if that data exists) — it should feel like a **quiet recognition moment.**

The tag design: a small, warm, slightly elevated badge. A tiny location pin icon (not just Material's default pin — ideally a slightly custom, warmer version) + the city name in bold compact sans. The whole badge has a slightly warm tint background — the pale version of the terracotta accent. It should feel warm, almost like a label someone affixed by hand.

Critically: the tag should never feel like a data field. It should feel like a handshake. "Oh, you're from Patna too."

## 9. What This Redesign Is Not

To make sure the redesign stays human and intentional, it explicitly avoids:

- **Glassmorphism**: No frosted glass cards. It looks dated and performs poorly on Android.
- **Gradient backgrounds or gradient buttons**: The palette is flat and confident. Gradients are a crutch.
- **Overanimation**: Not every element needs a motion state. Animation is used where it communicates, not where it impresses.
- **Dark mode as an aesthetic choice**: If dark mode exists, it should be a proper warm dark (not pure black backgrounds) — but it should not define the identity. Light mode is the default experience.
- **Notification badges on the home screen**: The home screen should not feel urgent or anxious. Badges, red dots, and count indicators belong on the notification tab, not bleeding into the feed.
- **Generic icon set**: All icons should be from a single consistent family. No mixing of outline and filled icons at the same level of hierarchy.
- **Uppercase everything**: Only used for category labels, time labels, and button text. Not for names, titles, or body text. Overuse of uppercase = overuse of emphasis.

## 10. What a Designer Should Deliver

If this brief is handed to a designer, the deliverables should be:

1. **Component library first**: Color tokens, type scale, spacing scale, icon choices, chip/pill variants, button variants, avatar styles, card surfaces. These should be defined before any screen is mocked.

2. **Home screen in 4 states**: Posts tab (default, with filter active, with search focused, empty state), Meetups tab (default, with date filter active, empty state).

3. **Carousel card**: all three RSVP states.

4. **Post card**: all connect button states × one post with image × one without × one expanded body.

5. **Meetup card**: all four RSVP button states.

6. **Filter bottom sheet**: default, one filter active.

7. **Motion spec**: keyframe values or Principle/ProtoPie prototype for the swipe transition, like animation, and stagger-in entrance.

8. **Figma frame** at 390×844 (iPhone 14 base) with an Android variant at 360×800.
